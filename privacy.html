Books Curator — Privacy Policy

Last updated 2 October 2026

Books Curator is a photography app for cataloguing books. It runs in your web browser on your own phone. Books Curator is provided by Paul Nicol, and where this page says "your curator" or "the curator", it means Books Curator. There is no account to create for the app, and it collects nothing about you beyond what this page describes. What your curator does once you share a folder with them, and the private library websites Books Curator can publish for a client, work differently: see After you share a folder with your curator and Private library websites.

The short version. The app keeps your photographs on your phone and in your own Google Drive. When you open the app, it asks your curator's request service (at script.google.com) which of your uploaded shelves have books to photograph. It sends only the Google Drive ids of folders the app created on your Drive (no photos, no names) and which kinds of request this version of the app can answer, and receives the author, title, where to find each book and what to photograph. It also tells the service which requests your phone has received, and your curator's list records that date. If you share your upload folder with your curator, see After you share a folder with your curator for what happens to it.

What the app stores, and where

  • On your phone. Your photographs (of shelves, and of any books your curator asks for), the details you type or dictate (shelf labels, notes, counts, copyright-page words, the condition you choose for a book's jacket and the book itself, your name), your curator's requests and your settings are kept in this app's storage in your browser on the device. Your photographs, details and settings stay there, also after an upload, until you delete them; the list of your curator's requests is refreshed each time the app checks. The photographs and the details you entered leave the phone only when you upload them into your own Google Drive (next point); if you dictate, your browser's voice service hears what you say (see below). The app saves each photograph as a fresh image, without location data.
  • In your Google Drive, if you choose. When you upload (tap Upload, or tap Next shelf within about an hour of signing in to Google), the app copies that shelf's or book's photographs into a folder in your own Google Drive account, with small text files of the details: the shelf label or the requested book's author and title, the notes, tags, count and copyright-page words you entered, the condition you chose, your name, and when you took the photographs. You own those files. When your curator asks again for a whole book, the app first moves the photographs it sent for the earlier request into a folder inside the book's folder named "_not this book" with the date (for example when they were of another book); nothing is deleted.

What the app can see in your Google Drive

The app requests a single, narrow Google permission: drive.file. This limits it to files and folders it creates itself, or that you explicitly hand to it. It cannot see, read, or list anything else in your Drive.

Sharing a folder

The app is set up to work with your curator. The first time you upload, it asks whether to share your upload folder with your curator's email address; the prompt shows the folder's name and the address.

  • Sharing is read-only — the other person can view and download, not alter or delete.
  • It happens only if you agree. Decline and nothing is shared.
  • It covers that folder and everything in it, including later uploads. Nothing else in your Drive is affected.
  • You remain the owner and can stop sharing at any time from Google Drive itself. Copies your curator has already made are not affected: see After you share a folder with your curator.

What the app sends over the internet

To the app's own address, and to Google:

  • bookscurator.net — the app's own files, hosted by GitHub Pages. Each time you open the app or return to it, it checks there for a new version. The app sends it nothing else.
  • accounts.google.com — to sign you in to your own Google account, when you upload, or when you open a book your curator asks you to photograph in full
  • www.googleapis.com — to upload your photographs to your own Drive, when you upload, and to read back the record of a book it sent before (that book's book.json), when your curator asks for the whole book
  • script.google.com — your curator's request service. Once you have uploaded a shelf, the app asks it whether your curator would like any of your books photographed: automatically, at most once a minute, when the app opens or comes back to the screen, and when you upload a whole book, to check that your curator still asks for it. It sends only the Google Drive ids of the folders the app created on your Drive and which kinds of request this version of the app can answer, and tells the service which requests your phone has received (your curator's list records that date). The answer comes back from script.googleusercontent.com.

Like any website, each of these sees your phone's internet (IP) address when the app contacts it. The app uses no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no other third-party services. Voice dictation, if you use it, is provided by your phone's browser and follows its own privacy terms.

Motion sensors

While the camera is open the app reads the phone's tilt sensor to draw a level line in the viewfinder. The readings are used on the spot and never stored or sent.

Deleting your data

Delete any shelf from the home screen, or everything the app holds from Settings → Delete ALL app data on this phone. The app then asks whether to also forget which shelves this phone uploaded: unless you say yes, it keeps that list (the Google Drive folder ids of the shelves and books this phone uploaded, with their labels and titles, and which requests you opened or answered) so your curator's requests keep appearing. Deleting a single uploaded shelf or requested book removes its photographs and details from the phone but leaves its Drive folder id, with its label or title, on that list, so your curator's requests for it still appear. Photographs already in your Google Drive are yours to keep or delete there; copies your curator has already made stay in your curator's records: see After you share a folder with your curator.

To withdraw the app's access to your Google Drive, remove Books Curator from the third-party connections in your Google Account. Removing the app from your home screen may leave what it saved in your browser: use Delete ALL app data first, and say yes to each question it asks.

After you share a folder with your curator

Once your folder is shared, your curator's own tools read it to identify and document your books. They copy some photographs and details (such as shelf labels, notes, the name you entered and when you took the photographs) into your curator's own records. They send reduced copies of your photographs (of shelves, and of books your curator asked for), with the labels and any copyright-page words you entered, to an outside research service that reads them and writes short notes on the books for your curator. They may also look up titles and authors, never photographs, in public book catalogues and on the web. Stopping sharing ends your curator's access to the folder; copies already made stay in your curator's records. To ask about them, write to your curator or to the address below.

Private library websites

Books Curator can also publish the books it has documented for a client as a private library website, at its own address under bookscurator.net. This is separate from the app. Only the people on the library's list can open it: the client, anyone the client names in writing, and the curator, who can always open it. The pages are delivered by Cloudflare. Its sign-in service, Cloudflare Access, holds the list's email addresses and records sign-ins, including refused ones, with the email address, time, IP address and country: a record the curator can see. If you sign in with Google, Google shows what it will share with Cloudflare (such as your name, email address and profile picture) and asks first. A named viewer without a Google account can instead be set up to sign in with a one-time code, which Cloudflare emails to them. The library's pages and copies of its photographs, with their location data removed, are stored privately on Cloudflare R2 and shown only to people on the list, after they sign in. Someone taken off the list can keep access until their sign-in ends, at most 24 hours after it began. The sign-in uses Cloudflare's own cookies. The library pages themselves set none, and carry no valuations, analytics or advertising.

Deleting a library. When the client asks in writing, the whole library website is deleted within 10 business days: its pages, the website's copies of the photographs, its sign-in and list of viewers, its address, the file it was built from, and the built copy on the curator's computer. This cannot be undone. Deletion does not touch your photographs in your own Google Drive, or your curator's own records of your books, including copies of some photographs. Records Cloudflare has already made in the curator's Cloudflare account, such as past sign-ins and the email address (and name, if the sign-in gave one) of each person who has signed in, are not removed by this deletion, and a Google sign-in stays listed in your Google Account until you remove it there. Anyone on a library's list can also ask to be taken off it. To ask, write to your curator or to the address below.
