Books Curator — Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 September 2026

Books Curator is a photography app for cataloguing books. It runs in your web browser on your own phone. There is no account to create for the app, and no company collecting anything about you through it. Private library websites, which a curator can publish for a client, work differently: see Private library websites.

The short version. The app keeps your photographs on your phone and in your own Google Drive. When you open the app, it asks your curator's request service (at script.google.com) which of your uploaded shelves have books to photograph. It sends only the Google Drive ids of folders the app created on your Drive (no photos, no names), and receives the author, title, where to find each book and what to photograph. It also tells the service which requests your phone has received, and your curator's list records that date.

What the app stores, and where

  • On your phone. Your shelf photographs, the details you type (shelf labels, notes, counts, your name) and your settings are held in your browser's local storage on the device. They are not transmitted anywhere by the app.
  • In your Google Drive, if you choose. When you tap "Upload", the app copies that shelf's or book's photographs and a small text record into a folder in your own Google Drive account. You own those files.

What the app can see in your Google Drive

The app requests a single, narrow Google permission: drive.file. This limits it to files and folders it creates itself, or that you explicitly hand to it. It cannot see, read, or list anything else in your Drive.

Sharing a folder

Some copies of this app are set up to work with a curator's archive. In that case, the first time you upload, the app asks whether to share the Books Curator folder with a named email address, which is shown to you in the prompt.

  • Sharing is read-only — the other person can view and download, not alter or delete.
  • It happens only if you agree. Decline and nothing is shared.
  • It covers that folder alone. Nothing else in your Drive is affected.
  • You remain the owner and can stop sharing at any time from Google Drive itself.

What the app sends over the internet

Only to Google:

  • accounts.google.com — to sign you in to your own Google account, when you tap Upload
  • www.googleapis.com — to upload your photographs to your own Drive, when you tap Upload
  • script.google.com — your curator's request service. Once you have uploaded a shelf, the app asks it whether your curator would like any of your books photographed: automatically, at most once a minute, when the app opens or comes back to the screen. It sends only the Google Drive ids of the folders the app created on your Drive, and tells the service which requests your phone has received (your curator's list records that date). The answer comes back from script.googleusercontent.com.

The app uses no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party services of any other kind. Voice dictation, if you use it, is provided by your phone's browser and follows its own privacy terms.

Motion sensors

While the camera is open the app reads the phone's tilt sensor to draw a level line in the viewfinder. The readings are used on the spot and never stored or sent.

Deleting your data

Delete any shelf from the home screen, or everything the app holds from Settings → Delete ALL app data on this phone. The app then asks whether to also forget which shelves this phone uploaded: unless you say yes, it keeps that list (the shelf labels and book titles) so your curator's requests keep appearing. Photographs already in your Google Drive are yours to keep or delete there.

Private library websites

A curator can also publish the books they have documented for a client as a private library website, at its own address under bookscurator.net. This is separate from the app. Only the people on the library's list can open it: the client, anyone the client names in writing, and the curator, who can always open it. The pages are delivered by Cloudflare. Its sign-in service, Cloudflare Access, holds the list's email addresses and records sign-ins, including refused ones, with the email address, time, IP address and country: a record the curator can see. If you sign in with Google, Google asks before sharing your name, email address and profile picture with Cloudflare. A named viewer without a Google account can instead be set up to sign in with a one-time code, which Cloudflare emails to them. The library's pages and copies of its photographs, with their location data removed, are stored privately on Cloudflare R2 and shown only to people on the list, after they sign in. Someone taken off the list can keep access until their sign-in ends, at most 24 hours after it began. The sign-in uses Cloudflare's own cookies. The library pages themselves set none, and carry no valuations, analytics or advertising.

Deleting a library. When the client asks in writing, the whole library website is deleted: its pages, the website's copies of the photographs, its sign-in and list of viewers, its address, the file it was built from, and the built copy on the curator's computer. This cannot be undone. Deletion does not touch your photographs in your own Google Drive, or your curator's own records of your books, including copies of some photographs. Records Cloudflare has already made, such as past sign-ins in the curator's Cloudflare account, are not removed by this deletion, and a Google sign-in stays listed in your Google Account until you remove it there. Anyone on a library's list can also ask to be taken off it. To ask, write to your curator or to the address below.
